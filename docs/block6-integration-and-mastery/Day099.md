# Day 99: Mental Models & Meta-Learning

**Block:** Block 6: Integration & Mastery · Meta-Learning

> **Goal of the day:** You have a written, evidence-based learning system for the next 12 months — based on active recall, spaced repetition, deliberate practice, and the Feynman test — and a concrete weekly review plan that protects the 100-day curriculum from being forgotten.

---

## 📖 Theory

100 days of input are worthless if the knowledge is gone in 6 months. Learning psychology (since Ebbinghaus 1885) is unanimous: **forgetting is the default operation of the brain**, retaining is the exception that only occurs through targeted retrieval practice. This day is the *meta-day*: it preserves the investment of the other 99.

### Concept 1: The forgetting curve & spaced repetition

Ebbinghaus showed: without repetition you lose ~50% after 1 day, ~70% after 1 week, ~85% after 1 month. The countermeasure is **spaced repetition** — repetition at growing intervals (1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 21 days → 60 days). Every successful retrieval *strengthens* the trace; every repetition *after* forgetting is inefficient.

The algorithm (simplified, like Anki SM-2):

```
if recall_quality >= 3:
    interval *= ease_factor   (default 2.5)
else:
    interval = 1
    ease_factor -= 0.2
```

The principle, not the tool, matters. Paper flashcards work just as well as Anki — but *only* if the repetition happens consistently.

### Concept 2: Active recall > re-reading

The most underestimated finding of learning research (Karpicke & Roediger 2008): **re-reading produces an illusion of competence, retrieval produces competence**. Read a chapter 5× → you feel competent, but you are not. Try to recapitulate the chapter *from memory* 1× → you feel bad, but you learn 2-3× more.

Concrete application to this curriculum:

- Instead of "read day 4 again" → "write down without a template what the ownership-borrowing rules are, then compare with day 1."
- Instead of "read through the code example" → "write the example from memory, compile, fix errors."

### Concept 3: Deliberate practice (Ericsson)

"Practice makes perfect" is wrong — *deliberate* practice makes better. Four elements separate ordinary practice from deliberate practice:

1. **Specific goal:** not "practice Rust" → "today: correctly annotate lifetimes in struct method signatures, 5 examples".
2. **Feedback loop:** immediate correction (compiler, test, reviewer) — not "look later".
3. **Difficulty in the sweet spot:** neither trivial nor frustrating. Just above current level.
4. **Repetition of weaknesses:** focus on what you do *wrong*, not what you can already do.

90% of engineers "practice" by reproducing finished things. That is not learning, that is confirmation.

### Concept 4: The Feynman test

Richard Feynman's method to separate understanding from illusion:

1. Choose a concept (e.g. "ML-KEM-768 envelope").
2. Write it down as if you were explaining it to a 12-year-old — no jargon words that you cannot define yourself.
3. Where you get stuck or use jargon → that is the gap. Back to the sources, fill only the gap.
4. Iterate until the explanation stands without a jargon joker.

Application: after every block (day 20, 35, 55, 75, 90, 100) write a Feynman essay on the block topic. Whoever cannot explain "lifetimes" without "borrow checker" has not understood them.

### Concept 5: Interleaving & transfer

Block-wise learning (1 week only Rust, then 1 week only TS) feels efficient, but is *interleaving*-inferior. Alternating repetition of different topics produces more robust retrieval paths: "today day 1 ownership, day 21 C pointers, day 56 AES-GCM" — connections between blocks emerge that purely sequential plans never create.

### Why this matters

This day is the *least technical* and *most transferable* day. The learning system applies to every future skill — next language, next domain, next career level. Engineers who internalize this learn 5× more in 10 years than engineers without a system — at equal time investment. This is the lever that counts most over 30 years.

### Pitfalls

- "Anki is enough" → without active-recall quality of the cards, spaced repetition is a waste of time. A bad card = "What is ownership?" with the book definition as the answer. A good card = forces self-production.
- Re-reading as a learning feeling → the most productive anti-learning. Retrieval feels *worse* and is *better*.
- "Practice = write more code" → without sweet-spot difficulty and feedback that is repetition, not learning.
- Skipping the Feynman test "because I can anyway" → the illusion is *the* symptom the test uncovers.

## 🛠️ Practice

**Task:** Build your learning system: 1 spaced-repetition setup, 1 weekly retrieval plan, 1 Feynman essay on the entire 100-day curriculum.

**Step by step:**

1. **Choose an SRS tool:** Anki (desktop free, mobile paid), Mochi, or physical flashcards. Decision criterion: the tool you will *still* use in 12 months. Standard anchor: Anki.
2. **Create 50 cards** — about half a card to one card per day of the curriculum (focus on blocks 1-5, not block 6):
   - *Retrieval card (good):* "Write a function that receives a string via `&str` and returns a new string, without clone. [Answer as a code image]"
   - *Understanding card (good):* "Why does `String` move, but not `i32`? (Answer in your own words, not the book formula)"
   - *Application card (good):* "Applying ML-KEM-768 *directly* over the payload — why is that wrong? What is the correct pattern?"
3. **Weekly retrieval plan** — `docs/learning/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md`:

   > **Hints:**
   > - **Format:** a fixed 45-min Sunday session split into active recall (write the 3 key concepts from memory), SRS reviews (~20 cards), a Feynman mini (1 concept in 5 sentences for a 12-year-old), and gap-notebooking for next week's cards.
   > - **Rotation (interleaving):** a table mixing blocks across weeks (e.g. Rust ownership + crypto, TS + PQC, C/Zig + AI/K8s, edge + capstone) — at least 4 weeks, then rotates.
4. **Feynman essay on the whole curriculum** — `docs/learning/FEYNMAN_OVERVIEW.md`: in 800-1200 words, explain the entire 100-day curriculum to a 12-year-old — what ownership, low-level tradeoffs, AI agents, PQC, and edge computing are and how they connect. No jargon without self-definition. Where you get stuck → mark the gap, look it up, fill only the gap.
5. **Gap collection file** — `docs/learning/GAPS.md`: all concepts where the Feynman test failed. Becomes input for next week's new SRS cards.

**Definition of Done (DoD):**

- [ ] SRS tool chosen and installed (Anki/Mochi/cards) — URL or photo noted in `docs/learning/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md`.
- [ ] At least 50 SRS cards created, of which ≥ 20 *retrieval cards* (production, not definition).
- [ ] `WEEKLY_REVIEW.md` has format + 4-week interleaving rotation.
- [ ] `FEYNMAN_OVERVIEW.md` has 800-1200 words, no undefined jargon.
- [ ] `GAPS.md` has at least 3 marked gaps from the Feynman essay.
- [ ] First weekly retrieval session completed today (45 min, documentation in `docs/learning/log.md`).
- [ ] You can explain in 2 sentences why re-reading is less efficient than retrieval (self-tested via the Feynman test).
- [ ] Commit: `git commit -m "day99: learning system — 50 srs cards + weekly review + feynman overview"`.

**Extension (optional):** Set up a 12-month calendar repetition (e.g. every 90 days a block Feynman essay, every 30 days a mini retrieval session). Enter the dates into the calendar *now* — "enter later" never happens.
