# Day 100: Final Review & 5-Year Roadmap

**Block:** Block 6: Integration & Mastery · Synthesis

> **Goal of the day:** You have (1) an honest self-assessment of all 6 blocks with documented gaps, (2) a written 5-year plan with 3 concrete yearly milestones, and (3) a "what I really learned" retrospective that does not describe the curriculum, but the transfers to future systems.

---

## 📖 Theory

Day 100 is not a celebration — it is the *accounting*. Two sources of error are typical here: (1) the self-assessment is too lenient ("I can do everything"), (2) the roadmap is too fluffy ("will get better"). Both mistakes make the next 5 years useless. This day forces quantification and concreteness.

### Concept 1: The honest self-assessment

For every block, rate yourself on a 4-point scale (not 1-10, that suggests false precision):

- **0 (unknown):** no real understanding, only buzzwords.
- **1 (reproductive):** can reproduce definitions/code examples that are in the curriculum.
- **2 (applying):** can apply concepts to new problems, diagnose errors.
- **3 (creative-synthesizing):** can connect concepts with other domains, justify new designs, teach others.

Rate every block honestly, *including* the gaps you found in the Feynman test (day 99). A "2" on block 4 means: you can apply PQC migration, but not innovate. That is a realistic senior baseline.

### Concept 2: Gap-aware roadmap

A roadmap without admitted gaps is a wish list. Link gaps to future learning goals:

```
Gap (from self-assessment) → Next learning goal → Timeline
- Block 2 Zig: 1 (reproductive) → Async Zig + kernel module project → Q3
- Block 5 RISC-V: 0 (unknown) → Bare-metal hello on RISC-V → Q4
- Block 3 MLOps: 2 (applying) → Own fine-tuning with a real dataset → Q1-Y2
```

Every gap becomes a concrete Q goal, not a "maybe sometime".

### Concept 3: The 3-level 5-year plan

5-year plans fail on over-detail. Use 3 levels with decreasing resolution:

- **Year 1 (detailed, quarterly milestones):** capstone v1 → OSS releases → first consulting engagements.
- **Year 2-3 (role goals):** senior engineer track OR independent consulting with a stable retainer.
- **Year 4-5 (direction, not job):** principal/staff track OR own small product/company, with niche = systems + security + AI-edge.

The resolution decreases because the world will be different in 5 years — but the *direction* must be set today, otherwise you drift.

### Concept 4: The "transferable lesson" section

The curriculum was never about Rust, Zig, or PQC — it was about *systems thinking*. The most valuable output of day 100 is the list of *transferable* skills that remain valid in any future tech-stack landscape, even if Rust/Zig/TS were obsolete in 5 years:

- **Contract-first thinking:** spec before code, error story before logic.
- **Risk-first planning:** name risks before setting milestones.
- **Mock-first testability:** traits + mocks → deterministic tests even for complex systems.
- **Envelope vs. direct crypto:** hybrid envelopes instead of direct encryption.
- **SBOM as a contract:** reproducibility + signature + SBOM = production-code maturity.
- **Scope cutting:** MVP + explicitly written-out V2.0 as disciplined craft.
- **Retrieval > re-reading:** a learning system that holds for 30 years.

This list is the *true* yield. Preserve it in the repo.

### Concept 5: The anti-delusion check

Before you commit the roadmap, run through 3 realism checks:

1. **Time-budget check:** did you really invest 1 h/day over the last 90 days? If no, reduce the roadmap volume before you call it a "plan".
2. **Compounding check:** is the year-5 goal *derived* from year-1 activities (causality), or is it a "would-be-cool-if" statement (hope)?
3. **Walk-away check:** what condition would make you abandon the roadmap? If you cannot name it, it is not a roadmap, it is a dream.

### Why this matters

This day decides whether the 99 previous days become a *durable competence curve* or a "did-that-once" memory. Engineers who continue their learning career without this reflection collect 10 years of experience 1× — engineers with this reflection collect 10 years of compounding experience. The difference is not talent, it is meta-discipline.

### Pitfalls

- Self-assessment too lenient → the Feynman test from day 99 is the source of truth; if you didn't do it, this assessment is worthless.
- Roadmap as a wish list without gap linkage → will never be executed.
- 5-year plan too detailed in years 4-5 → the world changes, detailed plans break, frustration follows.
- No walk-away criterion → the roadmap is not verifiable/falsifiable, thus indistinguishable from a real plan.

## 🛠️ Practice

**Task:** Write `docs/REVIEW_100.md` (self-assessment + transferable lessons) and `docs/ROADMAP_5Y.md` (5-year plan with gap linkage and anti-delusion check).

**Step by step:**

1. **Self-assessment** — `docs/REVIEW_100.md`, section 1:

   > **Hints:**
   > - **Self-Assessment table:** all 6 blocks, each with a 0–3 score (0 unknown → 3 creative-synthesizing), an evidence artifact, and the biggest gap. Be honest — use the day-99 Feynman gaps as the source of truth.
   > - **Transferable Lessons:** write out the 7 core skills from Concept 4 in your own words (contract-first, risk-first, mock-first, envelope crypto, SBOM-as-contract, scope cutting, retrieval > re-reading) — don't just list keywords.
   > - **What I would have done differently:** 3 concrete points.
2. **5-year roadmap** — `docs/ROADMAP_5Y.md`:

   > **Hints:**
   > - **Year 1:** 4 quarterly milestones (capstone maturation → first income pathway). **Year 2-3:** a role goal (senior/principal track OR stable consulting). **Year 4-5:** a direction, not a job (decreasing resolution).
   > - **Gap Linkage table:** connect each self-assessment gap to a learning goal and a concrete Q goal (e.g. Zig async → kernel module → Q3 Y1).
   > - **Anti-Delusion Check:** answer the 3 questions in writing (time-budget, compounding causality, walk-away condition).
   > - **Next concrete action:** a *single* task executable tomorrow — not a list.
   > - Use your own goals and timeline — the structure above is the shape to follow, not content to copy.
3. **Run the anti-delusion check** — answer the 3 questions in writing, honestly. If an answer comes out negative, adjust the roadmap (reduce volume, change track) before you commit.
4. **"Next concrete action"** — the roadmap ends with *one* next action executable tomorrow. Not 5 actions, one.

**Definition of Done (DoD):**

- [ ] `docs/REVIEW_100.md` exists with a self-assessment table (all 6 blocks, score 0-3, evidence artifact, gap).
- [ ] 7 transferable lessons are written out (not just keywords).
- [ ] `docs/ROADMAP_5Y.md` has year 1 with 4 quarterly milestones, year 2-3 as a role goal, year 4-5 as a direction.
- [ ] Gap-linkage table connects every self-assessment gap to a Q goal.
- [ ] 3 anti-delusion questions answered in writing, not empty.
- [ ] "Next concrete action" is a single task executable tomorrow (not a list).
- [ ] You can justify in 2 sentences why "10 years of compounding experience" ≠ "10 years of experience 1×".
- [ ] Commit: `git commit -m "day100: final review + 5-year roadmap with anti-delusion check"`.

**Extension (optional):** Publish a public "100 days retrospective" article (Substack/Dev.to/LinkedIn) that shows the self-assessment table — including gaps. Public gap transparency is the strongest credibility currency and simultaneously a retrieval event (Feynman test with an audience).
